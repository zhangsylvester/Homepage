Sierra & Sea

Ten ways to loop from Culver City to the giant sequoias and back — five 4-day routes and five 3-day routes, every day tuned to 4–6 hours behind the wheel. Sequoia National Park is the anchor of all ten; the variable is what you pair with it: Yosemite, the Kings Canyon gorge, the Central Coast, the Kern River backcountry, or an Ojai wind-down.

Book-now checklist: lodging in Three Rivers and Oakhurst (July weekends genuinely sell out) · a digital park entrance pass on Recreation.gov · Hearst Castle tour if you pick 4C · Kern raft trip if you pick 4D/3D · check NPS road & trail alerts the week you go (Crystal Cave tours and CA-190 status change year to year). No gas is sold inside any park — fill up in Visalia, Fresno, or Oakhurst.
🐾 Traveling with the Maltese — the one rule that shapes everything: the national parks (Sequoia, Kings Canyon, Yosemite) ban dogs on all trails — leashed pets are limited to paved roads, campgrounds, picnic areas and viewpoint pullouts, and can never be left in a parked car (it's 100°F in the foothills). The national forest is the loophole: the Trail of 100 Giants and Dome Rock welcome leashed dogs among the sequoias. Every option below carries a paw-print verdict; 4E is built dog-first, and 4D, 3D and 3E are the friendliest of the rest. One July hazard worth knowing: foxtails in dry grass are a real danger to a 10-lb dog's paws, ears and nose — stick to paved paths and check the pup after walks.
